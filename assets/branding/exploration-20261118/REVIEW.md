## Directions

| Choice | Style | Strength | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Counterpoint | Geometric monogram with a shared cap bar | The strongest compact header and icon reading | Most restrained personality |
| B — Imprint | Original serif JT paired with Fraunces lettering | Editorial character associated with an independent label | Fine details need a dedicated tiny-size cut |
| C — Cutline | Original chamfered alphabet | Bold name recognition and a consistent construction | Harder character than the other directions |
| D — Signed | Script initials and a flowing full-name signature | Personal producer identity | The initials and underline become delicate at 16px |
| E — Pocket | Rounded lowercase lettermark | Friendly reading and strong silhouette | Softer attitude than C |

A is recommended for everyday website use. B is the strongest alternative if the seller prefers a more editorial character. The choice remains with the seller.

## Inspection and refinement

Three drawing/render rounds preserved the earlier iterations. The second centered B, D and E optically. The last corrected C's custom K to exact 45-degree diagonals. The final comparison image and test screenshot were both rendered with Chrome and visually inspected.

The SVG audit found no structural issues in A/B/C/E symbols; D has a complexity advisory for its script contours. All lettering is vector paths, with no live text, raster, gradients, filters or external font dependency in the logo SVG files. The presentation board uses live text for its labels only.

Audit warnings on the font-derived wordmarks concern natural typeface angles and the number of outline points. These are not claimed as resolved to a zero-warning audit: snapping the original typeface contours to a rigid grid would damage the letterforms. C's custom alphabet has no remaining near-angle warnings, with an informational complexity notice for the full ten-letter name.

The first comparison includes actual 64/32/16px symbols. All five reversed previews were inspected. A/C/E retain the most robust small-size forms; B and especially D require dedicated small-size refinement if selected. This is concept testing, not a claim of final production readiness or trademark clearance.

No reference mark was copied. Library examples were used to study category conventions and construction; the test sheet includes owner-trademarked reference logos solely for comparison. Added typeface sources and official license links are recorded in BRIEF.md, with local license files in sources/.

After selection, the next stage is final geometry and optical refinement, then an approved kit of color/reversed versions, horizontal and stacked lockups, small-size artwork, favicon/web icons and a short usage guide. That stage is pending the seller's choice and request.
