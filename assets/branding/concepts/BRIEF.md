# JT logo exploration

JT is short for Joel Tracks. It sells R&B, Afro and Trap beat licenses to independent artists and labels. The logo must work in the website header, as a small player identifier and as a favicon.

The working character is direct, rhythmic and assured. These are reversible design assumptions based on the music catalog and the request for an eye-catching, readable interface. The existing text treatment has prototype value, with no established brand equity supplied.

Category research used the logo-design library’s media-entertainment group and exemplary lettermarks. The reference files were studied for construction, without tracing. Microphones, headphones, equalizer bars, a generic play triangle and concentric vinyl grooves were excluded from the concepts.

## Longlist

Scores below are internal comparisons out of 5 for clarity, distinction, simplicity, relevance and small-size strength.

| Idea | Type | Score |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Common Time — JT shares a single top bar while the J turns below | Connected lettermark | 23 |
| Afterbeat — lowercase j and t have opposing terminal turns | Lowercase lettermark | 22 |
| Two Takes — offset track corners define one open center | Abstract symbol | 21 |
| Open Session — J cuts an open counter through a frame | Negative-space symbol | 18 |
| Trackline — the Joel Tracks name uses a custom continuous baseline | Wordmark | 18 |
| J Loop — a J encloses a central timing notch | Letterform | 17 |
| Cut Point — angled terminals hint at the meeting of two tracks | Lettermark | 17 |
| Studio Stamp — JT within a square record-label stamp | Emblem | 16 |

The first three were built on a 256-unit canvas with 40-unit primary stems and visible gaps. Common Time uses straight stems and one J return, Afterbeat adds round returns and a detached dot, and Two Takes uses two corner shapes.

These are original concept drawings for a selection checkpoint. The site’s final logo and brand kit follow the selected direction. Concept files use filled SVG paths and need no font.

A library comparison does not establish trademark availability. A professional clearance search is needed before adopting a logo commercially.
