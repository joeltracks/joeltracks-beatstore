Nonempty saved dashboard wording takes precedence. These are original commercial recommendations for review, not a complete lawyer-approved contract or a claim about a universal industry standard. The original publishing allocation is 50% artist / 50% JT. New-song master royalty schedules are 90% artist / 10% JT for major-label artists and 70% artist / 30% JT for smaller indie artists. Custom price and splits are negotiated. Your seller editor can change local draft settings; saved settings are the values shown in the prototype. Master ownership, eligibility, revenue basis, deductions and accounting remain unconfirmed.

## What the sources support

Ear2ThaBeat's store uses an add/select-license/cart flow, a purchase guide and a four-tier comparison. Its lower fixed tiers list sales and stream caps, with broader limits at higher tiers. Its prices, file packages, royalty description and Exclusive offering differ from JT's confirmed structure. These are practices of one seller, rather than proof of an industry-wide standard. [Reference store](https://ear2thabeat.com/beat-store/)

Its FAQ describes upgrades and continuation of prior leases after a later exclusive sale. It also conflicts with its store comparison: the store's Unlimited card allows unlimited music videos, while the FAQ describes one. The FAQ's Exclusive summary says the beat can still be sold, while the store card and another FAQ answer say it is removed from sale. The FAQ's Unlimited description mentions a ten-year duration. Those inconsistencies prevent treating its marketing pages as a settled contract. [Reference FAQ](https://ear2thabeat.com/faq/)

The U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes compositions from sound recordings. Its beat-store guide identifies format, use, quantity and duration restrictions, describes many beat licenses as nonexclusive, and advises reading the full terms, including sample clearance and producer royalties. Tier names do not establish the permission by themselves. [Music copyright overview](https://www.copyright.gov/engage/musicians/) · [Beat-store guide, pages 8–9](https://www.copyright.gov/music-modernization/educational-materials/Sampling-Interpolations-Beat-Stores-and-More-An-Introduction-for-Musicians-Using-Preexisting.pdf#page=8)

YouTube requires exclusive rights for Content ID references and identifies nonexclusively licensed content as ineligible matching material. This is a platform rule about reference registration, distinct from permission to upload or monetize a licensed song. [YouTube reference requirements](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/107008)

All linked sources were accessed November 18, 2026. The Copyright Office guide itself is revised December 2021; it has not been represented as newly published. The reference's full executed contracts were not supplied or verified. No other seller's agreement text was copied.

## Proposed JT commercial schedule

These numbers and permissions are editable prototype defaults selected for display at your request; they do not activate licensed rights. The copy and stream steps use the reference as a comparison point. The video counts, view limits, performance treatment and reserved uses below are JT recommendations. Using the same ten-year worldwide term across the fixed tiers is a starting point for review, not a legal requirement or a verified industry standard.

| Permission | Basic | Premium | Unlimited | Custom |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| New songs per beat license | One | One | One | Negotiated |
| Paid copies / downloads | 5,000 | 10,000 | No unit cap | Negotiated |
| Audio-only streams | 100,000 | 500,000 | No audio-stream cap | Negotiated |
| Music videos | One | Two | No music-video cap | Negotiated |
| Monetized video views | 250,000 | 1,000,000 | No video-view cap | Negotiated |
| Live performances | No performance-count cap | No performance-count cap | No performance-count cap | Negotiated |
| Radio / TV broadcast | Separate written clearance | Separate written clearance | Separate written clearance | Negotiated |
| Film, ads and games | Separate sync agreement | Separate sync agreement | Separate sync agreement | Negotiated |
| Term / territory | 10 years / worldwide | 10 years / worldwide | 10 years / worldwide | Negotiated |
| Exclusivity | Nonexclusive | Nonexclusive | Nonexclusive | Case-by-case |

The common live-performance treatment avoids maintaining a separate show counter for an independent artist. Reserving broadcast and other sync uses allows placement-specific review. The proposed Unlimited tier removes the stated numeric caps; it would not transfer beat ownership, grant every possible use, or erase the confirmed royalty obligations.

## Original draft framework for counsel

The following outline is original, incomplete proposal wording for lawyer review. Bracketed matters require decisions. Checkout reads the editable dashboard fields for the selected tier; this outline explains the remaining legal drafting work.

1. **Parties and recording.** Identify JT's legal contracting entity and the artist's legal name, the beat ID/version and one resulting song. List any co-producers, writers and pre-existing rights. State the license fee, currency, selected file package and the conditions for the license to take effect.
2. **Proposed grant.** JT would permit the artist to incorporate the identified beat into one new song for the expressly listed uses and limits in the approved schedule. Fixed tiers would be nonexclusive. Any exclusive arrangement would require its own negotiated writing addressing earlier licenses and reserved rights.
3. **Assets and limits.** Deliver only the formats included in the approved tier. Count copies, audio streams and monetized video views across the agreed outlets for the identified song. Define short clips, duplicate uploads, counting sources and reporting before adoption. Reaching a cap would require contacting JT for new terms before exceeding it; no automatic upgrade fee or termination mechanism has been decided.
4. **Proposed restrictions and credit.** The artist would not resell the raw beat or stems as standalone assets or authorize unrelated users to reuse them. Proposed credit is “Produced by Joel Tracks.” Content ID reference registration using shared beat material would be restricted, with any distributor handling or matching exclusions expressly reviewed.
5. **Royalties and ownership.** Set out publishing and master royalty schedules separately, including each percentage's recipient. Define the master ownership arrangement, royalty base, allowed deductions, statements, payment timing, collection, registration, accounting and audit provisions. None of these unresolved definitions can be inferred from the supplied percentages.
6. **Duration and reserved uses.** The proposed ten-year worldwide term would start on an agreed effective date. Define renewal, existing releases at expiry, termination and remedies. Broadcast, film, advertising and game uses would need separate clearance under the proposal. Confirm any venue, platform or other third-party permissions relevant to the intended release.
7. **Other essential terms.** Address sample and interpolation clearance, representations, liability, breach and cure, refunds, transfers, upgrades, notices, governing U.S. state law and dispute forum. The seller's actual location and contracting entity must be known before counsel settles jurisdiction. A U.S. audience alone does not select the governing state.
